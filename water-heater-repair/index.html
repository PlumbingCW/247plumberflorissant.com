Water heater repair for Florissant homes

Water heater repair in Florissant, MO starts with a diagnosis, not a part swap. A heater that gives lukewarm water, runs out fast, makes popping noises or leaks can have six different causes, and only a few of them are worth paying to fix on an older tank.

We repair gas, electric, tankless and hybrid units. Most failures come from a short list: heating elements, thermostats, gas control valves and thermocouples, anode rods that have been used up, and sediment that builds in the bottom of the tank.

Emergency water heater problems: leaks and gas smell

A tank that is actively leaking or a heater that smells like gas needs attention right away. If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility or 911 first, and then call us once you are outside. A leaking tank is a water emergency we treat at any hour.

For a leaking tank, shut off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater and turn off power at the breaker for an electric unit, or turn a gas unit's control to pilot or off. Place a bucket under any steady drip and move stored items off the floor.

  • Gas smell: leave, call the gas utility or 911, then call us.
  • Water around the base: close the cold inlet valve above the tank.
  • Electric unit: switch off its breaker before touching the unit.
  • Open one hot faucet to relieve pressure only if the tank is not scalding.

Common causes of no hot water

Gas heaters often fail at the thermocouple or the pilot assembly. Electric heaters fail at the upper or lower element or the thermostat. Hybrid and tankless units add their own control boards and flow sensors.

  • Pilot goes out on a gas unit: thermocouple, draft or gas control.
  • Water is hot, then cold in minutes: failed lower element or heavy sediment.
  • Rumbling or popping: mineral sediment cooking at the tank bottom.
  • Rust colored hot water: anode rod spent and the tank lining under attack.
  • Water at the base: a fitting you can often repair, or the tank itself, which you cannot.

Missouri river water and your water heater

About 80 percent of Missouri American Water's supply for this region comes from the Missouri River, and the rest from the Meramec River. Its 2023 report lists total hardness of 82 to 226 ppm at the Missouri River plants, which is roughly 5 to 13 grains per gallon.

Water in that range leaves mineral scale on heating elements and settles as sediment in the tank. Annual flushing and checking the anode rod are the cheapest ways to extend heater life here. Source: Missouri American Water 2023 water quality report.
