Clogged Plumbing Vent Pipe Symptoms in Florissant, MO Homes

Gurgling sinks and slow toilets are not always a drain clog. Sometimes the problem is on the roof.

By the Florissant 24/7 Plumbing team, 2026.

Call (314) 461-6239

What a plumbing vent does

Every drain in your house needs air. A vent pipe runs from the drain system up through the roof and lets air in behind the water as it leaves, so water flows smoothly and sewer gas escapes outdoors. When a vent clogs, the system cannot breathe.

Leaves, a bird nest, snow or a failed vent boot can block the top of a vent. Inside the wall, a damaged section or an improper repair can restrict airflow too. The result looks a lot like a drain clog, which is why people reach for the plunger when the real problem is overhead.

Symptoms that point to a blocked vent

One symptom alone is not proof. Several together, especially after a storm or on a stack on the roof, raise the odds that a vent is involved.

  • Gurgling sounds from a sink or tub when another fixture drains.
  • Toilets that flush slowly or make a glugging noise.
  • Slow drains in several rooms with no hair or grease to explain them.
  • Sewer smells inside the house rather than outside.
  • Water in a fixture trap that is sucked out and leaves the trap dry.

Safe checks you can do

Look at the roof vents from the ground with binoculars. You are looking for obvious blockage such as a nest or debris. Do not climb a wet or steep roof, and do not stand on a roof in winter. If you see a clear blockage, a plumber or a roofer can clear it safely.

Inside, pour a few quarts of water into a dry floor drain or a seldom-used sink and listen for gurgling. If you hear it, the vent system may be restricted. A garden hose run down the vent can sometimes flush a light blockage, but only from a safe roof position, and it is a job better left to a pro.

When to call a plumber

Call if the symptoms persist after checking the vent top, if more than one fixture is affected or if you smell sewer gas. A plumber can run a camera down the vent, find the blockage and tell you whether it is a vent clog or a blocked main line. If the main is the issue, our sewer line cleaning service is the right next step.

How a plumber clears a blocked vent

The first step is a look from the roof or a ladder at the vent opening. Leaves, a bird nest and ice are the usual finds, and they come out by hand. If the opening is clear, a technician lowers a small camera or a cable down the stack to find a blockage further down.

A garden hose flushes loose debris, and a drain cable breaks up packed material. If the pipe is crushed, sagging or disconnected inside the wall, clearing will not hold and the section needs repair. We explain which case you have and show the camera view where we can.

  • Visual check of every roof vent opening.
  • Camera or cable run down the stack.
  • Hose flush to move loose debris.
  • Pressure and flow test at the fixtures afterward.

Vent trouble that starts inside the house

Not every vent problem is on the roof. After a bathroom or kitchen remodel, a drain may have been tied in without a proper vent, and the fixture then gurgles every time it empties. Some homes use air admittance valves under sinks, and those small valves can stick shut with age.

A vent that is too long, too narrow or run with too many bends also restricts airflow. These issues often show up only when a new fixture is added, so mention any recent work when you call. Our plumbing installation team checks venting before any new fixture is connected.

Keeping vents open through the year

None of these steps takes long, and together they cut down the calls that start with a gurgle at midnight. If a drain slows or a stack smells, call the line before the problem builds.

After storms

Look at roof vents from the ground for fallen leaves and branches.

In hard freezes

Moisture in the vent can frost over at the opening and shrink it, so a quick check after a cold snap helps.

Near trees

Trim branches that hang over a vent so leaves and nests stay out.

At roof work

Ask roofers to leave vent boots and screens intact and sealed.

What a vent repair can cost you in time and money

A vent cleared from the roof is among the cheapest plumbing fixes, because it needs a ladder, a hose and a short visit. A vent that has to be repaired inside a wall costs more, since drywall must be opened and closed again. We tell you which category yours is before any work starts.

Ask whether the quote includes a camera check of the vent after clearing. It confirms the stack is open all the way down, and it is the only way to be sure the gurgling will not return.

Why gurgling should not be ignored

A gurgle is air fighting to get into a drain, and that air has to come from somewhere. Sometimes it pulls through the water seal in a trap, and that seal is what keeps sewer gas out of your house. Over time a siphoned trap lets odors into the room, and in a closed bathroom those odors are more than a nuisance.

Catching a vent problem early also protects the drain itself. A partial vent blockage increases the strain on slow drains and can mask a real clog. Treat gurgling as an early warning, check the simple causes and call if it keeps up.

Questions to ask your plumber about a vent

A plumber who can answer these has actually inspected the vent and not guessed. Keep the notes with your home records, because a future buyer may ask about vent work.

  • Where is the blockage and how did you find it?
  • Can you show me the camera view or a photo?
  • Is the vent code compliant after the repair?
  • What did you do to keep it from returning?

What to know in Florissant

Florissant grew from 1,369 residents in 1940 to 38,166 by 1960, so many homes were built in the same decades. They may share vent stacks, boots and roof flashings that are now near the end of their service life. A cracked boot also lets water into the attic, which is a second reason to look.

The city lists plumbing work among residential projects that need a permit through Public Works. A simple vent clearing does not need one, but a rerouted vent or new stack does. Ask Public Works if you are unsure.
