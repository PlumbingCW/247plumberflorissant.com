Kitchen Faucet Leak Repair for Florissant, MO Homeowners

A kitchen faucet leak has only a few possible sources. Finding the right one saves you from buying the wrong part.

By the Florissant 24/7 Plumbing team, 2026.

Call (314) 461-6239

Where the leak starts matters

Dry the sink and cabinet, then run the faucet and watch. A leak from the spout points to the cartridge or seats. A leak around the base points to a worn O-ring. A leak under the sink points to supply lines, the shutoff valves or the drain connections.

Before you change anything, shut the valves under the sink and mop up. A flashlight and a dry paper towel on the pipes will show a slow drip you cannot see.

A simple cartridge replacement

Shut water off

Close both valves under the sink, then open the faucet to release pressure.

Remove the handle

Look for a set screw under a cap or behind a decorative button.

Pull the cartridge

Note the orientation and take it to the store for an exact match.

Reassemble and test

Reverse the steps and turn the water on slowly.

When to replace the whole faucet

If the faucet body is corroded, if parts are no longer made or if the finish is peeling, replacement costs less than chasing repairs. A modern faucet with a ceramic cartridge also tends to last longer. While you are under the sink, it is cheap to replace the shutoff valves and braided supply lines at the same time.

When the leak is not the faucet

A leak that shows up under the sink even when the faucet is off may be a drain or disposal connection. If a garbage disposal is attached, see our garbage disposal repair page, and for clogged drains see drain cleaning.

Find where the leak is

A dripping spout, a wet base and a puddle in the cabinet are three different problems. Dry the faucet and the sink deck with a towel, run the water and watch where the drops first appear. Check the supply lines and shutoff valves under the sink too, since a loose fitting can look like a faucet leak.

Knowing the location tells you the part. A drip from the spout usually means a worn cartridge or seat. Water at the handle points to an O-ring or packing. A wet base points to a seal or a loose mounting.

Common faucet types and their wear parts

A repair kit costs little, and a careful technician swaps a worn part in a short visit. Parts for older faucets can be hard to find, which can push the choice toward a new faucet.

Repair or replace

If the faucet is old, corroded or the finish is peeling, a new faucet is often the smarter spend. If the body is sound and only a seal has failed, repair is the lower-cost route. We can give you both numbers before you decide.

Hard water leaves scale on aerators and inside cartridges, and that wear is common in this area. Cleaning the aerator is a simple first step. For work beyond that, see our faucet and sink repair service, and ask about shutoff valves while we are under the sink.

Tools and prep for a do-it-yourself repair

Turn off the shutoff valves under the sink first, and open the faucet to release pressure. Take photos as you disassemble so you can put it back in the same order. If a nut will not move, stop and call, since forcing it can crack the faucet body or twist a supply line.

  • Adjustable wrench and a basin wrench for tight nuts.
  • Screwdrivers and a hex key set for handles.
  • Replacement cartridge or seal that matches the brand and model.
  • A towel in the sink drain so small parts do not fall in.
  • Plumber's grease for new O-rings.

Signs the faucet is beyond repair

In these cases a new faucet is the better spend. Pick a model from a maker that stocks parts for many years, and keep the manual so you know the cartridge number. A faucet that is easy to service saves you a call down the road.

  • The finish is flaking and exposing corroded metal.
  • The body is cracked or pitted.
  • Parts are no longer made for the model.
  • Repairs have been done more than once in a year.

Preventing the next leak

Clean aerators every few months to remove scale, and wipe the faucet base dry after use so water does not sit under the trim. Do not overtighten handles or lean on the spout. If you have hard water, soak the aerator in vinegar for an hour and brush off the deposits.

Replace supply lines when they are old, particularly braided ones with visible fraying. A burst supply line is one of the most common causes of cabinet flooding, and a new line takes minutes to install.

What to know in Florissant

Missouri American Water's 2023 report for the St. Louis County system lists total hardness up to 226 ppm at its Missouri River plants, which is roughly 13 grains per gallon. Hard water leaves mineral scale in aerators and cartridges, which makes drips and weak flow more likely over time.

Cleaning the aerator every few months and replacing a worn cartridge as soon as it drips are the habits that keep a kitchen faucet leak-free in this water.
