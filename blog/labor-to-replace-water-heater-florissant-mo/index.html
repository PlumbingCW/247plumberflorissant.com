Labor to Replace a Water Heater: A Florissant, MO Owner Guide

Labor is more than hooking up two pipes. Here is what you are paying for.

By the Florissant 24/7 Plumbing team, 2026.

Call (314) 461-6239

What labor covers

Replacement labor includes draining and disconnecting the old tank, removing it, setting the new one, making water, gas or electrical connections, installing a relief valve and discharge line, restoring venting and testing. A good crew also checks the expansion tank and shutoff valves.

The visit is more than a swap. It is a safety check of gas, venting and water pressure at the point where those systems meet.

Extras that change the labor

None of these are padding, but each should appear as a line item so you know what you are paying for. If you are moving to a different fuel, see water heater installation.

  • Rusted or failing shutoff valves.
  • Corroded or non-compliant venting on a gas unit.
  • Missing relief discharge line.
  • Tight space or an attic location.
  • Adding an expansion tank.
  • Switching fuel or moving the unit.

What the labor covers

Replacing a tank water heater is more than swapping the tank. The technician shuts off water and fuel, drains the old unit, disconnects it and moves it out. The new tank is set, connected to water, vent and fuel or power, and checked for leaks and proper draft.

Code items add steps. A new tank often needs a fresh temperature and pressure relief valve and discharge pipe, an expansion tank where the system is closed and proper venting. These are the parts that protect your home, and cutting them is where cheap quotes cut corners.

What changes the labor time

A straightforward swap with good access is the fastest. A job that involves venting changes or new gas piping takes longer and costs more, and we point that out at the visit.

  • The tank is in a tight closet or up a steep basement stair.
  • Old shutoff valves that will not close or old fittings that break.
  • Venting that no longer meets current rules.
  • Switching from one fuel type to another.
  • A permit and inspection are needed.

Safety checks that belong in the labor

These checks protect the people who live in the house. If a quote does not mention them, ask whether they are included. A careful installer treats them as routine, not as extras.

  • Combustion and draft test on gas units.
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve piped to a safe drain point.
  • Gas leak check at every new fitting.
  • Seismic or sturdy platform where required.
  • Thermostat set to a safe temperature.

Should you replace it before it fails

A water heater that is more than ten years old is living on borrowed time, especially if the water is hard and the tank was never flushed. Planning a replacement lets you pick the size and the time. An emergency swap at night costs the same labor but takes away your options and often adds water damage.

Warning signs include rust-colored hot water, rumbling noises, a damp base and a relief valve that drips. If you see any of them, schedule the replacement now.

Safety checklist before the plumber arrives

Gas and hot water bring real hazards, so a few rules help.

  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911.
  • Do not relight a pilot you cannot reach safely.
  • Shut off the water to the heater if it is leaking.
  • Keep children away from hot surfaces.

What to know in Florissant

Florissant requires a residential permit for water heater replacement, handled through Public Works. A properly permitted job is documented and inspected where required, and it helps at resale and in any insurance conversation.

Local water also affects the life of your tank. Missouri American Water's 2023 report lists hardness up to 226 ppm at the Missouri River plants, so ask the installer about a flush schedule and an anode rod check for the new unit.
