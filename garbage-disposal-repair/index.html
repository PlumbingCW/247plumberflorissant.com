Garbage disposal repair in Florissant, MO

Garbage disposal repair in Florissant, MO starts with a quick diagnosis: is it a jam, a power fault, a leak or a failed motor? Many humming units are simply jammed, and a hex key at the bottom of the unit or a reset button often brings them back.

A disposal sits at the junction of the sink drain, the dishwasher hose and the electrical supply, so a leak or a clog there can come from several places. We find which one and fix it instead of replacing a working unit.

Disposal emergency: leaks and sink backups

A disposal that leaks from the body, floods the cabinet or backs up into both sink bowls is an urgent problem. Turn off the wall switch and the breaker before touching anything near it, and never put your hand into the opening, even when it looks off.

If the dishwasher connection is leaking, stop running it until we check the hose and clamp. Dry the cabinet floor and place a pan beneath the unit.

  • Wall switch and breaker off.
  • Never reach into the disposal opening.
  • Stop the dishwasher if its hose connects to the unit.
  • Photograph any leak location for the record.

Why disposals fail

A humming unit with no grinding is jammed or has a stuck flywheel. A silent unit may have a tripped overload, a failed switch or a dead motor. A leak from the bottom means the seals have failed, which generally means replacement, while a leak at the top flange is usually a gasket or mount fix.

What you can safely put through a disposal

The City of Florissant lists wipes, paper towels, grease and oils among items that should never go down household drains. The same advice protects both your disposal and the sewer lateral that follows it.

  • Small food scraps with plenty of cold water.
  • Not grease, oil or fats, which coat the lines further down.
  • Not coffee grounds in quantity, potato peels or fibrous stalks.
  • Not wipes, paper towels or flushable items of any kind.

Installing or replacing a disposal

A new disposal means a new mounting assembly, a power hookup, a dishwasher knockout opened if needed and a leak test. We install new units or ones you provide. If there is no switch or outlet below the sink, we advise on the wiring needed. If the sink itself is being replaced, see plumbing installation.

A quick check before you call

If a jam clears and the unit runs, you are done. If it hums and stays stuck, or leaks at the body, the repair is likely a replacement. Never put a hand into the unit, even with the power off.

  • Turn off the power at the switch and breaker.
  • Use the hex key slot under the unit to turn the motor by hand.
  • Press the reset button on the underside of the unit.
  • Look under the sink for drips at the flange, drain and dishwasher hose.

Repair or replace

A leak at the sink flange or a drain connection is usually a simple reseal. A leak from the body of the disposal means the housing has failed and the unit needs replacement. A motor that has burned out is often not worth repairing on a basic model.

We can install a new disposal in the same visit in many cases. If a new switch or outlet is needed, read our blog guide to installing a disposal switch for how that part works.
