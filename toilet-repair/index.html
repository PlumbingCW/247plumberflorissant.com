Toilet repair and installation in Florissant, MO

Toilet repair in Florissant, MO covers clogs, leaks at the base or supply line, tanks that run or will not fill, wobbly bowls and full replacements. A toilet is a simple fixture with a few moving parts, and most problems come from a short list of worn parts.

Toilets are also the fixture people notice first when something larger is wrong. A toilet that keeps clogging may be a sign of a partial blockage downstream, and a toilet that rocks may be sitting on a rotted floor.

Toilet overflow emergency: stop the water

If a toilet is overflowing, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or close the shutoff valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise. This stops the flow within seconds. Then call us any hour.

Do not flush again. Towels at the door of the bathroom limit the spread, and if water is soaking through the floor, turn off power to rooms below.

  • Shutoff valve behind the toilet: turn clockwise.
  • No more flushing until we clear it.
  • Towels at the threshold of the room.
  • If several drains are slow, say so on the call, because the main may be blocked.

When the flange or floor is the real problem

A rocking toilet or a recurring base leak can mean a damaged closet flange or a rotted subfloor. Both are repairable. Catching a slow base leak early saves the floor, since moisture underneath weakens it for months before a stain appears.

  • Soft spot near the toilet base.
  • Water stains on the ceiling below.
  • A smell around the toilet that comes and goes.
  • A bowl that moves when you sit down.

Toilet installation and replacement

If the toilet is cracked, constantly clogs or uses a lot of water, replacement is the better choice. We set the new bowl on a fresh seal, check the flange, connect a new supply line and test several flushes.

Before choosing a new toilet, measure the rough-in distance from the wall to the flange bolts. It is the number that decides whether a model fits. For work around tubs and sinks, our faucet and sink repair and plumbing installation pages cover the next steps.

A toilet that rocks or leaks at the base

A toilet that moves when you sit on it has a loose bolt or a damaged flange, the ring that anchors the toilet to the floor. Water at the base means the wax ring or seal has failed. Leaving it can rot the subfloor over time, which is a much bigger repair than a new seal.

We lift the toilet, inspect the flange, replace the wax ring or switch to a modern seal and set the toilet level. If the flange is broken, we repair it before resetting the toilet.

Running tanks and weak flushes

These repairs are quick and inexpensive, and fixing a running toilet can reduce a high water bill. See how to find a hidden leak for the dye test.

  • The flapper no longer seals, so the tank keeps refilling.
  • The fill valve is stuck or noisy.
  • Rim jets are clogged with mineral scale.
  • The chain is too long or too short.

Safety checklist before the plumber arrives

Overflow water can carry germs.

  • Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet if the bowl is rising.
  • Wear gloves around overflow water.
  • Do not mix chemical cleaners.
  • Keep children away from the wet area.
