Old Town is the historic center of Florissant

The City of Florissant describes two Historic Districts, a Historic Residential district and a Historic Business district, at the center of the city beside City Hall. Exterior building design there is governed by the Old Town Guidelines, which the city developed to preserve its heritage.

French colonists settled the area and called it Fleurissant, and the Spanish later named it St. Ferdinand. The Old St. Ferdinand Shrine, with an 1819 convent, an 1821 church, an 1840 rectory and an 1888 schoolhouse, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Those dates matter to a plumber for one reason: buildings this old have usually been renovated more than once, so the pipes inside can be a mix of eras.

We do not know what material runs through your walls until someone looks. That is why we start with questions and, where it helps, a camera.

Where Old Town sits and how we describe it

The Old Town association places the area around a few anchor streets. The Old St. Ferdinand Shrine is at 1 rue St. Francois, Spanish Land Grant Park is a square bounded by St. Ferdinand, St. Denis, St. Charles and St. Louis streets, the Narrow Gauge Railroad Station is on St. Catherine Street, and Sacred Heart Catholic Church is on North Jefferson.

We do not publish an exact boundary, because we could not find one that residents and sources agree on. If you are not sure your address is inside the Historic District, the city's Public Works office can confirm it, and we can still serve you either way.

Plumbing work and the Historic District

The city's Landmark and Historic District Commission reviews exterior renovations of landmark properties and contributing historic buildings, along with public improvements in the district. Most plumbing happens inside the walls and is not an exterior change.

A few jobs can touch the outside, such as a new vent through a roof or wall that shows from the street, moving a meter or hose bib, or a repair that opens a facade. For those, ask Public Works whether review applies before work starts. We cannot answer that from a distance, and we would rather you ask first than redo work.

Plumbing services that fit Old Town homes and shops

Drains and sewers

Slow drains and backups in older buildings start with a look at the line. A sewer camera inspection shows what is there, and drain cleaning clears the blockage.

Water heaters

Tanks in older basements and closets age out. See water heater repair and replacement before you decide.

Leaks and supply lines

Stains, damp plaster and a rising water bill point to leak detection or, in a tired system, repiping.

Shops and restaurants

Small businesses in historic buildings have their own limits. See commercial plumbing for restrooms, floor drains and grease.

Before you call about an older building

Having these answers ready shortens the call and helps us explain the next step.

  • Find the main water shutoff and note whether it turns.
  • Take photos of any stain, leak or damaged pipe, with the date.
  • Note whether the building is a rental, a business or a house, and who owns it.
  • Check whether the work would change anything visible from the street.
  • Write down when the water heater was installed, if you know.

Old Town and the rest of Florissant

Old Town is one part of a city of about 52,500 people at the 2020 census, and most of its housing was built after the Second World War. For the larger picture, see our Florissant plumbing page and the towns and areas we serve. If you live farther east, Black Jack has its own page.
