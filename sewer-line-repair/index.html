Sewer line repair in Florissant, MO

Sewer line repair in Florissant, MO covers the pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the public sewer. When it cracks, offsets, collapses or fills with roots, you get slow drains, backups, wet yard patches or a sinkhole over the line.

Many homes here date to the 1950s and 1960s, when Florissant's population grew from 38,166 in 1960 to 65,908 in 1970. Sewer laterals installed in that era may be clay or cast iron, and joints in clay pipe are a favorite entry point for roots.

Sewer backup emergency: protect the house

Sewage in a tub, toilet or basement is a health risk and an emergency. Call us at any hour. Stop all water use in the house, keep children and pets away from the affected area, and do not run the washer or dishwasher.

Avoid contact with the water, wear boots and gloves if you must enter and shut off power to any basement outlets that are near the water. Report a sinkhole or cave-in to the city. The city arranges a dye test with the Metropolitan St. Louis Sewer District (MSD) to learn whether the break is in a public or private line.

  • Stop water use throughout the house.
  • Photograph the backup for your records and any claim.
  • Keep kids and pets out of affected rooms.
  • Call us, and report any sinkhole to the City of Florissant engineering office.

How we decide between repair options

The camera picture drives the choice. We show you the footage and explain why we recommend one path.

Spot repair

A short broken section is dug and replaced when the rest of the pipe is sound.

Trenchless pipe lining

A resin-saturated liner is cured inside the old pipe, avoiding a long trench through landscaping.

Pipe bursting or replacement

When a line is collapsed or badly bellied, a new line is needed.

Cleaning only

If the camera shows a clean pipe with a simple clog, a repair is not needed.

The City of Florissant sewer lateral program

Florissant runs a Sewer Lateral Insurance Program for residential properties. It covers defective sewer lines from the main sewer to within five feet of the home's foundation. Homeowners pay a $50 annual fee on their property tax bill and a refundable $300 application deposit, and a city plumber inspects the line by video.

Eligibility requires that the line was cabled by a licensed plumber or drainlayer without success, or that the city or MSD confirmed a sinkhole tied to the sewer line. Work inside five feet of the foundation and inside the home remains the owner's cost. Because of those rules, we document cabling visits and give you a written finding to bring to the city. Always confirm current program terms with the city.
