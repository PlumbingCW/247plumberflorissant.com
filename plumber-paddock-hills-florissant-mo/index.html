A postwar subdivision behind Parker Road

St. Louis Patina places Paddock Hills behind the Church of the Master off Parker Road in Florissant, and calls it the first postwar subdivision it covers in the city. It says the developer was the Mayer-Raisher Mayer company, which often built ranch houses, and that the subdivision had several different designs. Its photographs date from November 1959 and March 1960.

The name is also known well beyond Florissant. The Supreme Court case Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co., decided in 1968, grew out of a sale dispute in this subdivision. We mention it only because it shows that Paddock Hills is a documented name and not a label invented by a listing site.

For plumbing, the useful fact is simple. Homes from around 1960 are now in their sixties, so fixtures, water heaters and drain lines have had a long time to wear. The materials in any one house are unknown to us until we see them.

Streets, ZIP codes and an overlap we want to be honest about

Listing sites mostly file Paddock Hills under ZIP 63033, with a few addresses in 63031. Street names that appear under the Paddock Hills label include Parker Road, Tremont Drive, Hollywood Lane, Belair Terrace, Pacer Court, Filly Court, Palomino Lane and Furlong Lane.

We could not find a published boundary. Saint Benedict Lane and Saint Edward Lane appear in listings under both Paddock Hills and Duchesne Hills, so the edges are blurry. If your street is near the edge, tell us the street and ZIP and we will serve you the same way.

What to check in a house of this age

None of these needs a tool. They tell you whether you have a small repair or a bigger question.

  • Read the water heater data plate. The serial number usually shows the build date.
  • Note whether several drains slow down together, which points to the main line.
  • Look for stains on ceilings and around the base of toilets and the tank.
  • Find the cleanout, if there is one, and keep it clear.
  • Check the supply shutoff valves under sinks and toilets for drips.

Plumbing services for a ranch home

Water heaters

A tank in its second decade is a candidate for replacement. A younger one may only need repair.

Drains and the main line

Repeated backups call for drain cleaning and, if they return, a camera inspection.

Leaks

A hidden leak shows up as a stain or a rising bill. Leak detection finds the source before walls are opened.

Fixtures

Running toilets and dripping taps are usually quick fixes. See toilet repair and faucet repair.

When to call and when to wait

Call right away if water is spraying, sewage is backing up or a tank is leaking heavily. Shut off the main valve first and keep people away from wet electrical areas.

A slow drain or a dripping tap can wait for a scheduled visit. If you cannot tell which this is, call and describe it. We will say plainly what we think and what we cannot know from the phone.
