Burst pipe repair in Florissant, MO

Burst pipe repair in Florissant, MO is urgent because a split supply line can release dozens of gallons a minute. Fast action limits damage to drywall, flooring and belongings, and most of the cost of a burst pipe is the water, not the pipe.

We repair copper, PEX, CPVC and galvanized pipe and replace damaged sections. A burst after a hard freeze, a corroded elbow that fails or a bump from a renovation are the common stories we hear.

Burst pipe emergency: do these three things

First, shut off the main water valve. It is usually near where the water line enters the house, often in the basement. Second, turn off the water heater and, if water is near electrical outlets, cut power to that area at the breaker. Third, call us. We answer day and night.

Open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the remaining water out of the pipes. Move belongings and place towels or buckets to catch drips. Photograph the damage for your insurance claim before you clean up.

What causes pipes to burst in older Florissant homes

Frozen water expands and splits pipe in exterior walls, garages, crawlspaces and unheated rooms. Corrosion weakens older galvanized and copper pipe until a joint fails under normal pressure. Worn fittings and failed supply connectors cause bursts that are not weather related.

Pipe in homes from the 1950s and 1960s may be galvanized steel, which corrodes from the inside and restricts flow before it breaks. If one section bursts, others of the same age may be close behind, which is a point to consider before patching.

After the repair: drying and documenting

After the repair, wet drywall and insulation should be dried quickly to prevent mold. We can advise on what to document and whether the damage warrants a restoration company. Homeowners insurance often covers sudden water damage from a burst pipe, but the repair of the pipe itself is generally the homeowner's responsibility, so ask your agent.

After the water is off

Once the main is closed, open a faucet at the lowest and highest points to drain the lines. Move belongings away from wet areas and take photos for insurance. Do not use the wet area if outlets or the panel are nearby. If the ceiling is sagging with water, stay out of the room and call us.

The technician finds the failed section, cuts it out and replaces it with new pipe and fittings. We test the repair under pressure before closing any wall, and we point out other weak sections if the pipe is old.

Why pipes burst in this area

Water pressure above the usual home range puts extra strain on joints. A pressure gauge check is part of our visit, and a pressure reducing valve may be a fix. See water line repair when the break is outside the house.

  • Freezing in unheated crawl spaces and exterior walls.
  • Old galvanized lines that corrode from the inside.
  • High water pressure that stresses fittings.
  • Poor soldering or connection fittings from earlier repairs.
