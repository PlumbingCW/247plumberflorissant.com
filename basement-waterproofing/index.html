Basement waterproofing in Florissant, MO

Basement waterproofing in Florissant, MO starts with a diagnosis because water in a basement may come from rain outside, groundwater, a plumbing leak or a failing sewer line. These four causes need four different fixes.

Many homes here were built during the postwar years when Florissant's population climbed from 1,369 in 1940 to 38,166 in 1960. Basements from that era can be older block or poured walls with no interior drainage, so seepage and floor joint moisture are common concerns.

What we do and what we do not

We are plumbers. We install sump pits and pumps, interior drain connections, backwater valve and check valve work and plumbing repairs. We do not do exterior excavation or structural wall repair, and we will say so if your problem is structural. If cracks widen or walls bow, involve a structural professional.

  • Sump pits, pumps and backup systems.
  • Interior drainage connected to a pit.
  • Floor drain and backwater valve work.
  • Plumbing leak tracing and repair.
  • Downspout and discharge routing advice.

Cheap fixes to try first

These steps cost little and often reduce the moisture a basement sees. We tell homeowners honestly when exterior fixes will do more than interior work.

  • Extend downspouts several feet from the house.
  • Clean gutters before storms.
  • Keep soil sloping away from the foundation.
  • Check that dehumidifier drains are not leaking.
  • Test the sump pump before heavy rain.

A plumbing-first way to find the source

Most wet basements get a plumber's attention only after a drainage contractor has been called. We reverse that order because plumbing sources are the cheapest to rule out. We check supply lines, the water heater, washer hoses, floor drains and the main sewer line, then move to groundwater and surface runoff.

If a camera shows the main line is slow and the floor drain is the point of water entry, the fix is a sewer repair and not a drainage system. Ruling out those causes first protects your budget.

  • Dye test of the floor drain and the stack.
  • Meter test for a hidden supply leak.
  • Sump pit and pump inspection.
  • Camera inspection of the main drain.
