Repiping in Florissant, MO

Repiping in Florissant, MO means replacing the water supply lines inside a house with new pipe, usually PEX or copper. It is a planned project, not an emergency repair, and it makes sense when the old pipe leaks in several places, loses pressure or discolors the water.

A large share of Florissant homes were built from the mid 1950s to the 1960s, a period when the city grew from 1,369 people in 1940 to 38,166 in 1960 and 65,908 in 1970. Houses of that age may still have original galvanized steel supply lines, which rust from the inside and narrow over time.

Signs the pipes are due

One leak does not mean a repipe. A pattern of failures does.

  • Rust colored or brown water after the faucet sits.
  • Low pressure in several fixtures.
  • Leaks at more than one joint in a short time.
  • Pinhole leaks in copper.
  • Pipe that is flaking, stained green or white with deposits.
  • Metallic taste in water.

Partial repipe or full repipe?

If failures are limited to one run, such as a kitchen branch, a partial repipe may fix it. If the pipes are the same age and material throughout, a full repipe prevents repeated visits. After a burst pipe repair, we can price a partial plan next to a whole-house plan so you can compare. If you also want a new heater in the same project, see water heater installation.

Signs the pipes are the problem

One leak is a repair, and several leaks in a short period are a pattern. If you are not sure, a leak detection visit shows how many problem spots there are.

  • Rust-colored or brown water after the line sits unused.
  • Pressure that drops when two fixtures run.
  • Repeated pinhole leaks in different spots.
  • Visible corrosion or crusting at joints.
  • Pipe material your insurer or inspector flags.

Phased or whole-house

A whole-house repipe replaces all supply lines in a few days with the house usable at night. A phased approach replaces the worst run first, such as the kitchen or the basement trunk line, and spreads the cost. We discuss both with a diagram of what changes.

Walls need access holes, and we plan them in closets and hidden areas where we can, and patch the openings. Plan for a day or two without water at certain times, and ask us for the schedule in writing.

Safety checklist before the plumber arrives

Repiping opens walls and shuts off water, so a few precautions help.

  • Know where the main shutoff is.
  • Move furniture away from walls we will open.
  • Plan for water to be off at set times.
  • Keep children and pets out of work areas.
