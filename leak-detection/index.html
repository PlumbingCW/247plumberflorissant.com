Leak detection in Florissant, MO

Leak detection in Florissant, MO means locating a hidden water leak without tearing out finished surfaces. Most callers start with a symptom instead of a diagnosis: a higher water bill, a meter that keeps moving, a wet ceiling or wall, a warm spot on the floor or low water pressure.

We work from the symptom to the pipe. The goal is a pinpoint location, a clear explanation and a repair plan that opens as little as possible.

Active leak emergency: what to shut off

If water is visibly coming through a ceiling or you hear it running inside a wall, treat it as an emergency. Call us at any hour. Shut off the main water valve if you cannot find the source quickly, and keep power off in any room where water is near outlets or light fixtures.

A leak that continues unchecked can stain, swell and rot materials within a day or two, so a quick call matters even if the amount of water looks small.

  • Know where your main water shutoff is and test it now.
  • Photograph the damage and the meter reading for records.
  • Place a bucket under drips and lay towels along affected walls.
  • If a ceiling is bulging, do not stand under it. Poking a small hole at the lowest point can relieve weight, but only if it is safe.

Starting with the water meter test

A simple meter test tells you whether you have a leak at all. With every faucet and appliance off, check the meter's leak indicator or note the dial. If it moves, water is going somewhere.

A moving meter can mean a leak inside the house, in the yard service line or at a fixture such as a running toilet. We separate those by isolating sections of pipe, which narrows the search before any tools come out.

How we pinpoint a hidden leak

No single method finds every leak, so we combine two or three. Once located, we explain whether a pipe repair, a fixture fix or a line repair is the answer.

Acoustic listening

Sensitive microphones pick up the sound of pressurized water escaping a pipe.

Thermal imaging

A thermal camera shows cool or warm patterns behind finished surfaces.

Moisture mapping

Meters trace damp areas to the source and show how far it has spread.

Pressure isolation

We shut sections of pipe and watch for pressure loss to confirm the leaking run.

Where leaks hide in older Florissant homes

Florissant grew from 1,369 people in 1940 to 38,166 by 1960, so many homes are in their sixth or seventh decade. Supply piping in that era includes galvanized steel and early copper, and joints, valves and old fittings are where weeping starts. Under-house or crawlspace leaks, ceiling stains below bathrooms and wet basement walls near the plumbing stack are common starting points.

The water main outside matters too. Missouri American Water has been replacing cast iron mains installed between the 1940s and 1970s in Florissant, including work on Harrison, Washington and St. Catherine streets in 2022. That is a utility-side project, but it shows why a service line from the meter to the house deserves a test when a bill climbs.

What you can do while waiting for us

These steps limit damage and give us a starting point when we arrive.

  • Close the main valve if water is visible and spreading.
  • Photograph the meter and wet areas.
  • Move belongings off damp floors.
  • Avoid running appliances that use water.

Safety checklist before the plumber arrives

Active leaks can damage property and create slip and shock hazards.

  • Shut the main valve if water is visible and spreading.
  • Keep people away from wet electrical areas.
  • Move belongings off damp floors.
  • Photograph wet areas and the meter reading.
